🥈 What is Silver (Chandi) Rate in Pakistan?

Silver, known locally as Chandi, is one of the most traded precious metals in Pakistan's bullion and investment markets. The silver rate in Pakistan is determined by the international spot price (XAG), combined with local factors such as the USD to PKR exchange rate, import duties, and logistics costs.

For everyday investors and households, silver offers a more affordable entry point into precious metal investments compared to gold, making it popular among Pakistan's middle class and small-scale savers.

📊 Latest Silver Rate in Pakistan

The silver (Chandi) rate changes continuously with the international spot market. Local Sarafara associations in different cities announce their own daily reference rates, which can vary slightly by city and dealer.

Silver is measured in Tola (traditional Pakistani unit, ≈11.66 grams) and 10 Grams for wider market reference.

The rate cards and table on this page are calculated live from the international silver spot price and the current USD to PKR rate — always confirm with your local dealer before a transaction, as local prices include their own margin.

🌍 Why Silver Rate is Important in Pakistan?

  • Affordable alternative to gold for small investors
  • Widely used in traditional jewelry and ornaments
  • Growing industrial demand (solar, EVs, electronics)
  • Used by some as a hedge against PKR depreciation
  • Directly impacted by the USD/PKR exchange rate

Silver's dual role as both a precious metal and industrial commodity makes it a widely watched market indicator in Pakistan.

📈 Factors Affecting Silver Rate in Pakistan

1. Global Spot Price (XAG)

International silver prices set the foundation. Any shift in global supply-demand directly reflects in Pakistan's Sarafara market.

2. USD to PKR Exchange Rate

Since silver is imported, PKR depreciation against the dollar tends to raise domestic silver prices, all else equal.

3. Industrial Demand

Growing global use of silver in solar panels, EV components, and electronics can add to demand and price pressure over time.

4. Seasonal Demand

Wedding seasons can drive higher local jewelry demand, which may temporarily push local silver rates higher.

📉 Silver Rate History in Pakistan

Over the past two decades, silver rates in Pakistan have generally trended upward, driven by a combination of PKR depreciation, global industrial demand, and inflation.

🏭 Silver Market in Pakistan – Overview

  • Sarafara associations in major cities set their own daily reference rates
  • Major trade hubs: Karachi, Lahore, Rawalpindi
  • Investment-grade silver bars and coins are available from bullion dealers
  • Physical silver is generally preferred over paper instruments locally
  • Pakistan Mercantile Exchange (PMEX) offers regulated commodity futures

Pakistan's bullion market continues to draw interest from retail investors looking for alternatives to bank savings accounts.

💱 Forex Market & Currency Rates Pakistan

Pakistan's foreign exchange ecosystem operates through two key channels:

  • Interbank Market: Used by commercial banks, corporations, and the State Bank of Pakistan for official trade settlements.
  • Open Market: Used by retail consumers, exchange companies, and overseas remitters. Rates are more dynamic and demand-driven.
The State Bank of Pakistan monitors and periodically sets guidance on the spread between interbank and open market rates — check SBP's current policy for the exact figure, as it has changed over time.

💵 USD to PKR – Impact on Silver & Economy

Primary Reserve Currency

The US Dollar is Pakistan's primary anchor currency. Its movements directly affect import costs, petroleum prices, and domestic inflation.

Effect on Silver Prices

A weaker Rupee means higher import costs for silver in PKR terms, which tends to raise per-tola rates in Pakistan's bullion markets.

Remittances & Stability

Worker remittances from GCC countries (Saudi Arabia, UAE) in SAR and AED — both pegged to USD — represent an important stabilizing inflow for Pakistan's foreign exchange reserves.

💡 Silver Investment Guide for Pakistanis

Consider Physical Bullion Over Jewelry for Investment

Jewelry carries "making charges" that are typically lost at resale. For investment purposes, many buyers prefer high-purity silver bars or certified minted coins, which track the spot price more closely with a smaller premium.

Think in Terms of a Longer Horizon

Short-term silver trading can be impractical for retail investors given buy-sell spreads at local dealers. Many financial advisors suggest thinking in terms of a multi-year horizon rather than short-term moves, to ride out volatility and benefit from longer-term trends like industrial demand growth.

Currency Diversification

Beyond silver, some investors hold a mix of currencies (USD, EUR, GBP) alongside PKR savings as one way to manage exposure to currency depreciation cycles — this is a personal risk decision, not a guarantee against loss.

Buying During Market Dips

Many investors aim to accumulate during market corrections rather than at local peaks — but timing markets is inherently uncertain, and this is not a guaranteed strategy.

🌐 Global Macro Trends Affecting Pakistan's Silver Market

When the US Federal Reserve raises interest rates, capital often shifts from commodities toward US Treasuries, which can weigh on silver prices. In lower-rate environments, silver has historically tended to perform better, though this relationship isn't guaranteed every cycle.

Rising industrial demand — including from green energy and electronics manufacturing globally — is a widely cited factor supporting silver's longer-term relevance as a commodity, alongside its traditional role as a precious metal.

🏦 Open Market vs Bank Rate

Two main exchange rate systems operate in Pakistan:

  • Bank Rate: Official interbank rate used for trade documents and formal transfers
  • Open Market Rate: Retail rate used by exchange companies and currency dealers
Open market rates commonly differ slightly from interbank rates. Always compare current rates before exchanging large amounts.

Why does silver rate change every day?

Silver rates change continuously because they're linked to the international spot price and the USD to PKR exchange rate, both of which fluctuate throughout the trading day.

Is silver a good investment in Pakistan?

It can be one option for investors with a longer time horizon, as it may offer some hedge against PKR depreciation and inflation, alongside growing industrial demand. As with any investment, it carries risk, and this isn't personalized financial advice.

What is the difference between interbank and open market silver rates?

Interbank rates are used by banks for institutional transactions, while open market rates at Sarafara shops are what retail buyers actually pay — open market rates are typically somewhat different from interbank levels.

How is silver (Chandi) price measured in Pakistan?

Silver is priced per Tola (a traditional unit equal to approximately 11.66 grams) and per 10 grams for broader market reference in Pakistan.
