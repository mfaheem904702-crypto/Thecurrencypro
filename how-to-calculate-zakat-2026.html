1. Understanding the Core Principles of Zakat

Zakat is a mandatory annual levy of 2.5% (1/40th) assessed on net wealth that exceeds a specific minimum threshold called the Nisab, provided that wealth has been continuously held for one full lunar year (Hawl). Unlike general charity (Sadaqah), Zakat is not voluntary; it is an obligation for every adult, sane Muslim who possesses wealth equal to or above the Nisab.

To accurately calculate Zakat, you must categorize your wealth into Zakatable assets, non-Zakatable personal items, and allowable debt deductions. The core mathematical framework is structured as follows:

The Fundamental Zakat Formula

Net Zakatable Assets = (Total Gold & Silver Value + Cash & Bank Balances + Business Inventory + Investments) − (Immediate Liabilities & Short-Term Debts Due)

Zakat Payable = Net Zakatable Assets × 2.5% (or 0.025)

2. Detailed Breakdown of Zakatable Asset Categories

A. Gold and Silver Bullion & Jewelry

Gold and silver are Zakatable in all physical forms, including coins, bars, bullion, and jewelry. Whether worn daily or stored as an investment, Shariah rulings mandate evaluating gold and silver at their prevailing market scrap/buyback rates on your Zakat anniversary date.

  • 24K Pure Gold: Assessed at 100% pure market value per gram or tola.
  • 22K / 21K / 18K Gold: Must be converted to equivalent pure gold mass or valued at its specific purity market rate before applying the 2.5% rate.
  • Silver: Valued at the current silver market rate per gram or tola.

B. Cash, Bank Balances, and Cash Equivalents

All liquid monetary reserves are fully Zakatable. This includes physical cash on hand, savings accounts, checking accounts, fixed deposits, and foreign currency balances. Foreign currencies should be converted to PKR using live rates available on TheCurrencyPro Exchange Rate Hub.

C. Stocks, Mutual Funds, and Business Stock

For short-term trading stocks or crypto, evaluate at 100% current market value. For long-term dividend investments, Zakat applies to the net liquid assets of the business (typically 20% to 30% of total portfolio value).
