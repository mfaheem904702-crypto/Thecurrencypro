📌 Introduction: The Festive Remittance Phenomenon

Every year during Ramadan, Eid-ul-Fitr, and Eid-ul-Adha, millions of Non-Resident Pakistanis (NRPs) across the GCC, Europe, North America, and the UK send money home to support family spending, Zakat obligations, and festive preparations. This annual trend triggers massive capital movements into Pakistan's banking system.

However, overseas senders frequently observe that exchange rates fluctuate rapidly—sometimes daily—during the 15 to 20 days leading up to Eid. Understanding the core economic drivers behind these rate shifts enables senders to secure better conversion rates and avoid costly transaction delays.

💡 Key Takeaway: Exchange rates during Eid fluctuate due to liquidity demand, commercial exchange company margins, interbank supply spikes, and multi-day central bank closures.

1. Primary Reasons Why Remittance Rates Shift Before Eid

Several distinct market forces collide simultaneously during the festive season to shift exchange rates:

  • Massive Inflow Surge (Supply and Demand): The sudden influx of hundreds of millions of foreign dollars, riyals, and dirhams into the interbank market temporarily increases foreign currency supply, which can cause local currency appreciation or volatility.
  • Cash Liquidity Crunch: Banks and money exchange operators face an unprecedented demand for physical domestic currency (PKR cash) as families withdraw money for Eid shopping, Eidi, and Qurbani arrangements.
  • Wider Exchange Spreads by Operators: To protect themselves against exchange rate risks during upcoming long public holidays, money transfer companies often widen their exchange rate margins (the difference between buying and selling rates).
  • Central Bank Interbank Closures: When the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) and commercial interbank markets close for public holidays, exchange rates get locked or adjusted conservatively to prevent losses over extended non-trading days.

2. Remittance Rate Movement Matrix During Eid Season

Here is how currency rates and transfer conditions typically behave across different phases of the holiday season:

Early Ramadan (Days 1–15) Steady, normal transaction volumes Stable interbank alignment Fast (Same Day)
Late Ramadan (Days 16–25) Peak remittance activity begins Competitive rate offers from operators Normal (1–2 Days)
Pre-Eid Rush (Final 5 Days) Extreme volume, high cash demand Wider margins, potential rate drops Delayed due to queue overload
Eid Holidays (Actual Days) Interbank closed, emergency cash only Frozen/Fixed fallback exchange rates Digital Instant Only (RDA/Wallets)

3. How Overseas Pakistanis Can Get the Best Rate During Eid

To maximize the recipient value of your hard-earned money during the festive surge, follow these strategies:

  1. Avoid Last-Minute Transfers: Send money 7 to 10 days before Eid holidays begin. This protects your transaction from bank holiday cut-off dates and processing bottlenecks.
  2. Compare Online Transfer Apps vs. Physical Counters: Digital money transfer services and digital banking channels generally offer lower fees and narrower exchange spreads compared to physical exchange counters.
  3. Leverage Roshan Digital Accounts (RDA): Remitting directly into an RDA or local account via IBAN ensures instant clearing, official interbank conversion rates, and zero hidden exchange counter fees.
  4. Monitor Mid-Market Rates: Keep track of live interbank conversion rates using platforms like TheCurrencyPro before locking in a rate with your transfer service.
