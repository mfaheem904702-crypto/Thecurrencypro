Understanding Zakat on Modern Assets

Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam — an obligatory annual payment of 2.5% on wealth that has remained above the Nisab threshold for a full lunar (Hijri) year. While the concept is centuries old, applying it to modern financial life — bank accounts, stock portfolios, business inventory — sometimes needs a bit of clarification.

What Counts Toward Zakatable Wealth

  • Cash and bank balances: savings accounts, current accounts, and cash on hand all count in full.
  • Gold and silver: jewellery held as an investment or beyond personal use is included at current market value, though there is scholarly difference of opinion on jewellery worn regularly for personal use.
  • Business inventory and stock: goods held for trade are valued at their current market price, not their original purchase cost.
  • Investments and shares: the market value of shares held for trading purposes is generally Zakatable; shares held long-term for dividends may be assessed differently depending on the underlying business assets.

What Can Be Deducted

Short-term debts and immediate liabilities — money you owe that is due imminently — can be subtracted from your total wealth before calculating Zakat. Long-term liabilities like a multi-year mortgage are typically only deducted for the portion due within the coming year, according to most contemporary scholarly guidance, though practices vary between schools of thought.

Why the Nisab Threshold Matters

Zakat only becomes obligatory once total wealth exceeds the Nisab — the minimum threshold, traditionally defined as either 7.5 Tolas of gold or 52.5 Tolas of silver. Since gold and silver prices move independently, using the silver-based Nisab (which is almost always the lower value) means more people fall into the obligatory range, which many scholars view as the more cautious and charity-favoring approach.

A Note on Precision

This calculator provides a straightforward 2.5% calculation on the net wealth you enter. For complex cases — mixed business partnerships, agricultural Zakat (which follows different rules), or unclear debt situations — it's worth consulting a knowledgeable scholar or Islamic finance advisor for a precise ruling specific to your situation.

How Zakat is Calculated — Quick Reference

Zakat is an obligatory charity in Islam calculated at 2.5% of a Muslim's total cumulative wealth that stays above the Nisab threshold for one full lunar year.

  • Gold Nisab: 7.5 Tolas (approx. 87.48 grams)
  • Silver Nisab: 52.5 Tolas (approx. 612.36 grams)
  • Net wealth is calculated by adding all cash, bank balance, gold, silver, and liquid trade assets, then subtracting short-term debts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Zakat calculated in Pakistan? +
Zakat equals 2.5% of net wealth — cash, gold, silver, and liquid business assets minus immediate debts — held above the Nisab threshold for one lunar year.
What is the Nisab threshold for Zakat? +
7.5 Tolas of gold (about 87.48 grams) or 52.5 Tolas of silver (about 612.36 grams). Many scholars prefer the silver value since it is lower.
