Forex trading and the rules in Pakistan

Foreign exchange in Pakistan is controlled under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947, and the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) sets the framework. The Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) regulates exchanges such as the Pakistan Mercantile Exchange (PMEX).

Be careful with offshore platforms. Regulators have cautioned about unauthorised forex platforms, and sending money abroad to unlicensed brokers can create legal and financial problems. Read the latest SBP and SECP circulars and confirm a platform's licence before you deposit anything.

Do not confuse trading with ordinary currency exchange. Converting dollars to rupees at a dealer is a one-off transaction; trading means speculating on price movements, often with leverage.

Risk management basics

  • Use stop-loss orders so a losing trade is closed at a level you chose in advance.
  • Respect leverage. It magnifies losses as quickly as gains.
  • Watch the calendar. Interest-rate decisions, US jobs data and SBP policy statements can move prices sharply.
  • Never trade money you cannot afford to lose. Industry risk warnings commonly report that most retail traders lose money.

Frequently asked questions

Is forex trading legal in Pakistan?

The rules for retail forex trading are strict and not simple. Foreign exchange is controlled by the State Bank of Pakistan, and regulators have warned about unauthorised platforms. Check the latest SBP and SECP guidance before depositing any money.

What is PMEX?

The Pakistan Mercantile Exchange is an SECP-regulated exchange for futures contracts. Check its website for the products currently offered before you trade.

Is forex trading the same as exchanging currency at an exchange company?

No. Exchanging currency is a one-off conversion at a dealer's rate. Forex trading means speculating on price movements, often with leverage, and it carries a high risk of loss.

Why is USD/PKR called an exotic pair?

Pairs that include an emerging-market currency are usually called exotics. They tend to have wider spreads and react strongly to reserves, trade balances and policy news.

What is leverage and why is it risky?

Leverage lets you control a larger position with less capital, which magnifies losses as well as gains. A small adverse move can wipe out a deposit.
