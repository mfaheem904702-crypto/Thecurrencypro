Why Money Sitting Idle Loses Value

A common mistake is assuming that money kept in a low-interest or non-interest bank account is "safe" simply because the number never goes down. In real terms, it isn't safe at all — inflation steadily reduces what that money can actually buy, even while the balance stays fixed or grows only slightly.

This calculator shows the real value of a fixed amount of money after a chosen number of years at a given inflation rate — in other words, what that same amount would be worth in today's purchasing power terms, not in future nominal Rupees.

How This Plays Out in Pakistan

Pakistan has experienced periods of both moderate and quite high inflation across different years, often tied to currency depreciation, import costs (especially energy and fuel), and domestic supply conditions. Because these swings can be significant year to year, it's worth checking the latest inflation figure from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics or the State Bank rather than assuming a fixed long-term average when planning multi-year savings.

What This Means for Savings Decisions

If your savings account pays 8% annual interest but inflation runs at 12%, you are losing roughly 4% of real value every year — even though your balance is technically growing. This is why financial planning conversations in high-inflation environments often focus on real return (return minus inflation) rather than the nominal interest rate alone. Assets like gold, real estate, or foreign currency savings are sometimes used as partial inflation hedges, each with its own risks and trade-offs worth researching separately.

Using This Calculator Practically

Try running a few different inflation-rate scenarios (say, 8%, 12%, and 18%) over the number of years you're planning for — a wedding fund, a home deposit, retirement savings — to get a realistic range for how much purchasing power you'll actually need to plan around, rather than relying on a single assumption.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does inflation affect purchasing power? +
Inflation reduces how much a fixed amount of money can buy over time — if it isn't earning returns above the inflation rate, its real value falls every year even as the balance stays the same.
What has Pakistan's inflation rate typically looked like? +
It has fluctuated significantly across different years. For the current official rate, check the latest release from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics.
